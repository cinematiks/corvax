Why MICs have outperformed in a high-rate environment

Mortgage investment corporations are not new, but the current rate cycle has made them unusually relevant for income-focused investors.

When you want to invest your money to earn a steady income, you usually think of options like savings accounts, bonds, or stocks. However, there is another corner of the financial world called private debt. Inside this category, a specific type of investment known as a MIC—which stands for Mortgage Investment Corporation—has been getting a lot of attention lately.

MICs are not a new invention, but recent changes in the economy have made them highly relevant for income-focused investors. To understand why they are performing so well right now, it helps to look at how interest rates affect the way we borrow and lend money.

What is a Mortgage Investment Corporation (MIC)?

Think of a MIC as a giant pool of money collected from many different investors. A professional management team takes this pooled money and lends it out to people or businesses who need a mortgage to buy real estate.

In return for the loan, the borrowers pay interest. The MIC collects all of this interest, takes out a small fee for managing the fund, and then passes the rest of the profit directly back to the investors as regular income payments.

The Impact of a High-Rate Environment

Over the past couple of years, central banks have raised interest rates to help control inflation. When interest rates go up, regular banks become much stricter about who they lend money to. They might turn away perfectly good borrowers simply because their strict rules do not allow for flexible situations.

This is where MICs step in. Because they are private lenders, they can be more flexible than major banks. They can charge higher interest rates to compensate for the unique situations of their borrowers.

In a high-rate environment, MICs outperform for two main reasons:

  • Floating Interest Rates: Many of the loans that MICs hand out are short-term or have adjustable interest rates. When regular interest rates go up across the country, the rates on these private mortgages go up too. This means the MIC earns more money, which translates into higher payouts for the investors.
  • More High-Quality Borrowers: Because big banks are turning people away, higher-quality borrowers who just need quick or temporary financing are turning to private debt. This allows MICs to pick better, safer projects while still charging a premium rate.

What to Understand Before You Invest

While MICs can offer excellent returns when rates are high, they are not completely risk-free.

  • Real Estate Risk: Because the loans are tied to property, a massive drop in the real estate market could hurt the value of the loans.
  • Liquidity: Unlike stocks, which you can sell at any second on the stock market, your money in a MIC might be locked away for a set period. It is harder to pull your cash out instantly if you suddenly need it.

Summary

Mortgage Investment Corporations offer a unique way to earn income by acting like a private bank. In an economy where interest rates are high, their ability to charge more for loans allows them to outpace many traditional investments, making them a powerful tool for investors looking to grow their regular income.
